Tonicization - Applied Dominants Link to heading

==Tonicizing “Major” or “Minor” does not matter, they are the same==

Mini V-I or vii°-I other than the current key. Involve a V-I dominant -> tonic not in the home key. Root motion with descending 5th or ascending 5th Tonicizing chord must be in major or diminished (the part where it plays the dominant chord) no diminished keys.

Tonicization: momentarily treat a note other than 1̂ as a tonic, no cadence, no pivot chord.

In an applied dominant: A Chromatic chord that functions as a dominant of a harmony other than I (Use to resolve to a chord other than I)

Any major/minor triad can be tonicized be preceded by an applied dominant (aka secondary dominant): which contains a chromatic note and functions as a Dominant of a harmony other than I. When tonicize with diminished chord, it is not possible to make it sound like home key.

Any Tonicization e.g. The V/ii: minor triad whose root is a perfect fifth above ii The root of ii in the key of D major = E minor (minor because ii is minor) ii in the key of D major: E G# B V in the key of ii (E minor): B D# F#

Attention: All the four part harmonic rules still apply, Especially on the tendency tones since tonicization is a dominant-tonic motion. Link to heading

Major VI - I - V ii - vi - iii

Minor VI - III - VII iv - i - v

Applied Dominants of V Link to heading

Characteristic/Note of V(7)/V: Link to heading

Any chord that precedes diatonic V can precede V/V. e.g. tonic/subdominant then V or V/V

V/V must follow by V; obey V-I in the second dominant key harmony V/vi must follow by vi; obey V-I in the second dominant key harmony

Treat it like the V in the main key but with a special accidental

It can appear in inversion; can omit the fifth and double the root

The second dominant leading tone (raised 4̂) Link to heading

  • A leading tone in the key of V
  • Must resolve up to the tonic
  • Must not be doubled

V(7)/V in minor key requires two accidentals: raised 4̂ and raised 6̂

Leading tone Resolution Link to heading

Use the cadential 64 to delay the raised 4̂ resolution

Raised 4̂ slide down by half to natural 4̂; watch out for cross relation; Note that the 4̂ and raised 4̂ must be in the same voice registration

Other Applied Dominants Chords Link to heading

Only major and minor triads may be tonicized (treated like tonic) and be preceded by applied dominant. V/ii and not V/vii°. Since Tonicization requires a major or a minor chord to be home key (I or i).

Momentarily treating the goal chord as a tonic, resulting temporarily V-I. Like Tonicization with V/V

An applied dominant can be applied to any minor or major triad other than V. Hence V/ii leads to ii, V/vi leads to vi, etc.

All applied chords tonicize the chord to which they are applied

Uses the same Logic as Applied Dominants of V

Applied vii°6, vii°7, viiØ7 (in major only): Link to heading

They are dominant chords. Hence they can be served as applied chords to any major or minor triad

The root of vii°6 and vii°7 and viiØ7 is the leading tones of the harmonies they tonicize. Treat it as a leading tone

It requires the root and the tonic of the home is are m2 distance apart

Build the diminished 7th chord above that G# (if the chord starts with E G B, no accidental needed at the E)

Shift down applied V by third Link to heading

  • if # on third -> # on first + ♭on seventh (seventh♭may not be needed)
  • if # on third and # on fifth -> # on first and # on third
  • if♭on seventh -> ♭on fifth +♭on seventh
  • if no accidental -> ♭on seventh
