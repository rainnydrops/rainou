# The Classic Epic sound

1.  Prepare a four part writing.
2.  Assign each voicing to an instrument
3.  Expands from four part writing
    - Add octaves
    - arppegiator
    - play with the original notes with instrument property
    - etc.
4.  Do the same with wind and brass instrument
5.  Add in percussion for rhythm

## Cinematic string (another way of saying epic)

1.  four part writing
2.  **Fifth voice** is a filling line (no worry on parallel fifth) - **add body** to the piece
3.  **Sixth voice** for doubling bass line down a octave (can be divisi) (without filling)
4.  **Seventh voice** doubling soprano up an octave (divisi) (without filling)
5.  **Eighth voice** doubling top three voice up an octaves (divisi) (without filling)
6.  **Nineth voice** **(1)** Eighth voice with filler line in the middle **(2)** Copy the soprano down an octive to chello instrument.

## Orchestration tip to try out

1.  Clarinet and Bassons are the String Quartet of the Woodwinds  
    Oboe has different quality and sound (timbre)

> Oboe is the naughty boy of the orchestra

1.  Contrast is important, instead of contrasting string and woodwing, put the contrast on different timbre
2.  String as percussion
3.  Think of the music linearly over time: Sketch the line and adding dynamics to know what to do with each of the notes. (i.e. intensify a note -> stack the note with heavy chord) (how the whole orchestra supporting the line)
4.  Having a rich sound: start with four part chorale (see the upper two part)

Heterophony: Everyone plays the same thing but some have additional embellishment.

### Melody Texture: Activating Melody

- Klangfarbenmelodie: Same fragment that constantly comes back, a little bit like delay.
- Split Melody: Same melody, but different instrument with different part. wise
- Interlocking: Same melody, but different instrument with different note wise.
- Heterophone: Embellishment melody

### Harmony Texture: Activating harmony

- Repetition: Same harmony with repeat (i.e. eight notes repeat or other notes)
- Alternation: Same harmony, but on a alternate with different instruments.
- Arpeggio: Play arpeggio
- Gesture: Change of gesture: change of a hold note to an arpeggio

## Bow (arco)

### Played with hair

#### On-the-String

- slur (together under one bow - in the same direction)
- non legato
- legato
- detache
- loure/portato:
    - Fade out & fade in and continue in the same direction
    - Mark with a tenueto
- staccato
- martele
- marcato
- tremollo: moving back and forth very fast
    - measure tremollo: sub divided into rhythmic tremollo: each slash is multiplying the notes: 1 slash for 2 notes. 2 slash for 4 notes. 1 and 2 slash are usually measured, 3 and more slashes are not measured
    - Two type of tremollo
        - play very fast on the same string
        - play very fast between two notes
- Con sord: mute (put the rubber on the bridge to make it muted - less sounded) sensorsordino (mute come off)
- Scordatura: tune higher/lower intentionary

#### Off-the-String

- staccato
- spicatto
- saltando
- jete/rochet/rechochet: bounce on the string
- arpeggiando

### Played with wood (can damage the bow)

- **col legno tratto**: use partly of the wood (50%) and hair (50%). Specify if you want the wood only.
- **col legno battuto**: hit with the wood of the bow (can sound like rainning splash sound)

### Direction with the bow

- up-bow (on the upbeat)
- down-bow (usually on the down beat)

### Part of the bow used

- **la purta d'arco**, at the tip, a la pointe (airy)
- **al tailone**, at the frog, a la talon

### Contact point with string

- sul tasto
    - played on figerboard
    - muted
- sul porticello
    - Played close to the bridge
    - more harsh sounding
    - metallic glassy sound
    - Nasely
    - fight and flight response sounding

## String Harmony

- Nodes: places with zero amplitures (no movement) (at two end of the string)
- Anti-nodes: place with max amplitude (with most movement) (where the string vibrate the most)

![Screenshot 2024-09-18 at 16.50.43.png](5.png)


### How string vibrate

- First mode of vibration
    - regular play
    - Fundamential
    - pitch of the first mode of vibration eg. 98Hz for G
    - No division
- Second mode of vibration
    - vibrating twice as fast e.g. 196Hz (one octave higher)
    - Division in the middle
- Third mode of vibration
    - vibrating three time as fast e.g. 294Hz (D + 2 cents)
    - Division at 1/3 and 2/3 of the string
- Forth mode of vibration
    - vibrating three time as fast e.g. 392Hz (D + 2 cents) three octave higher
    - Division at 1/4 and 2/4 and 3/4 of the string
- There are infinite mode of vibration
- The way string plays basically triggers the different mode of vibration for each of the notes.

### String harmony

- For a harmony on string to produce a sound, the finger needs to be directly pressed on the nodes to produce a string harmonic sound.

### Notation
I-II-III-IV on string denote open string from highest to lowest
- Tableture: show where to put the finger
    - diamond notehead
    - show where the finger is placed
    - show what string
    - does not indicate the sounding pitch
    - Roman numeral represent the specific string on the instrument
- Sounding: Show the souding pitch
    - Uses regular notehead
    - Indicate sounding pitch
    - Should only be used when fingering is the same as sounding pitch
    - Only use if the fingering and the sounding note are the same

# Side Notes

- Timbre is defined by the top and the bottom note of the instrument in the sound contour/harmony: outside instrument becomes the predominant in the hearing (when bunch of the instruent are stacked together.)
- Wind instrument uses the same fingering system in order for the same wind player to be able to play different instrument. Because of that, the sounding pitch for every instrument are different. The sounding pitch of these instrument are called "Concert Pitch"
- the Shape of the instrument defines their sounding
	- Cylindrical bore (cylindre like tube) -> Brighter  
	- Conical bore (cone like tube) -> mellow Sound

# Brass Instrument

- To be categorized as brass instrument: the sound needs to be produed by the lip vibration: Brass instrument = Labraphones
- Without changing any valves, by modifying the mouth movement, the brass player can only plays harmonic series
- Because the air is vibrating directly with the tube. The sound produced by brass instrument is mainly played on a Harmonic series.
- Changing the length shift the Harmonic series.
- Every single harmonic series has gaps where the pitches are missing, brass relies on changing the length of the tubes to produce different harmonic series via valves (Trumpet, horns, tuba, trombone for extending its slide). All of them combined together to form a complete chromatic scale
- The longer the tube, the lower the note
- Prefered to play in flat keys

## Order on sheet music

1.  French horn
2.  Trumpet 1
3.  Trumpet 2
4.  Trombone
5.  Tuba

- Brass player can be specialized on a certain range -> Alto, Tenor or Bass
- When annotated as Alto, Tenor or Bass, it simply means that this part (whatever instrument) is mainly played on the range of Alto, Tenor or Bass.

The `x` above the note bring attention to the musician that this particular pitch needs adjustment. **But its best to avoid those notes altogheter**

By combining all of the harmonic series, brass player can produce all the different chromatic notes: There are 7 common harmonic series brass player uses

### Harmonic Changes/Switch

Traditional brass instrument does not have way to changes the harmonics, but modern instrument have incrporated to change different length tube so a player can freely changes between all the harmonic scales.

#### Trombone Harmonic

The higher position (longer tubing), the lower the pitch will be. In this case, the position depict the number of semitone it deduct from C pitch.

![Trombone position and semitone](36.png)


#### Trumpet Harmonic

Trombone uses slide to control the length of the tubing. Trumbet uses valve with each valve assigned with certain length of tubing to change the pitch of the note when pressed. Press valves 1 and 2 or 3 to gain hamonic series of A
